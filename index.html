01 · Course overview

Introduction

FIELD STUDY 1:

Observations of Teaching-Learning in Actual School Environment

Field Study 1 provides an opportunity for future educators to observe how teaching and learning take place in an actual classroom setting. This course allows field study students to connect the theories and principles they have learned during their college years with actual teaching practices and classroom experiences.

This e-portfolio presents the author's observations, experiences, and learning throughout Field Study 1. It begins with her Field Study Student (FSS) Biography and Philosophy of Teaching and Learning: Pre and Post Field Study, which reflect her background and beliefs about education. It also includes the Compilation of Field Study Worksheet Activities, Reflections and Insights from Field Study Observations, Evidence of Learning, Retrospection and Documentation, which present her accomplishments, personal reflections, and realizations during the course.

Through classroom observations, the author realized that teaching involves more than simply explaining lessons and giving activities. She was able to observe learners' behavior, motivation, and participation, as well as how her resource teacher applied different teaching strategies and classroom management techniques to address students' learning needs. As a future mathematics educator, she gained valuable insights into how lessons can be presented in ways that make mathematical concepts easier to understand and more meaningful to learners. She believes that the knowledge and experiences she gained from Field Study 1 will serve as a valuable foundation as she continues preparing for the teaching profession.

Andrea has made a personal promise to herself and her family to complete her degree on time. 03 · Reflective practice

Philosophy of Teaching and Learning: Pre and Post Field Study

A teacher's philosophy of teaching and learning reflects the beliefs and principles that guide classroom practices, instructional decisions, and relationships with learners. This section presents the author's beliefs before Field Study 1 and how these were strengthened, broadened, and developed through actual classroom observations and learning experiences.

Before · Initial perspective

Philosophy of Teaching and Learning: Pre-Field Study

Before taking Field Study 1, my beliefs about teaching and learning were mainly influenced by my experiences as a student and the knowledge I had acquired from my professional education courses. I already had certain ideas about what makes teaching effective, particularly in helping students understand lessons and develop their academic abilities.

Beliefs I held before the field study:

  • Teachers should possess sufficient knowledge of their subject matter and explain lessons clearly and systematically.
  • Students learn better when they are encouraged to participate, ask questions, and express their ideas during classroom discussions.
  • Learning Mathematics requires consistent practice, appropriate guidance, and opportunities to apply mathematical concepts.
  • A respectful and supportive relationship between teachers and learners encourages students to participate and improve their performance.
  • Well-prepared lessons, clear instructions, and suitable learning activities contribute to an organized and productive learning experience.

Initial views I had not fully considered:

  • I placed greater emphasis on how well a teacher explained and presented the lesson than on the different factors that could affect students' learning.
  • I tended to give more attention to correct answers and academic performance without fully considering the learning process, individual progress, and other ways students demonstrate understanding.
After · Informed by observation

Philosophy of Teaching and Learning: Post-Field Study

After completing my Field Study 1 observations and worksheet activities, I developed a broader perspective on the teaching and learning process. Observing actual classroom situations allowed me to recognize that effective teaching involves not only mastery of the subject matter but also thoughtful planning, appropriate instructional practices, and consideration of learners' individual needs.

Beliefs strengthened and developed through Field Study 1

Episodes 1, 5, 6, and 7

A safe, organized, and supportive learning environment contributes to students' concentration, participation, and overall learning experiences.

Episodes 2 and 3

Every learner has different abilities, interests, experiences, and backgrounds; therefore, teachers should provide appropriate guidance and learning opportunities that consider these differences.

Episode 4

The family, school, and community have significant roles in learners' development, making cooperation and communication among them essential in supporting education.

Episodes 8, 9, and 10

Effective teaching requires clear learning objectives, appropriate instructional strategies, and assessment activities that are aligned with the intended learning outcomes.

Episodes 9, 10, and 16

Students develop meaningful understanding when they actively participate in discussions, collaborate with others, solve problems, explain their reasoning, and connect lessons with real-life situations.

Episodes 12 and 13

Assessment should not focus solely on grades but should also provide feedback, identify learning difficulties, monitor progress, and guide improvements in teaching and learning.

Episode 11

Technology and instructional materials can support effective learning when selected and used appropriately according to lesson objectives and learners' needs.

Episodes 14 and 15

Teaching requires professional competence, ethical behavior, adaptability, and continuous learning to respond effectively to the changing needs of students and education.

Clarifying and improving my previous understanding:

  • Effective teaching is not measured only by how clearly a teacher delivers the lesson. Teachers also serve as facilitators who guide students in developing understanding, reasoning, problem-solving skills, and independence.
  • Students' learning cannot be determined solely through correct answers, examination results, or numerical grades. Their participation, improvement, demonstrated skills, and ability to apply what they have learned are equally important indicators of learning.

As a future mathematics educator, I now believe that teaching should balance mastery of essential mathematical concepts with learner-centered activities that develop critical thinking and meaningful understanding. These realizations have strengthened my belief that education should prepare learners not only to succeed academically but also to apply their knowledge and skills beyond the classroom.

06 · Looking back

Retrospection

Looking back on my Field Study 1 experience, I can say that the 16 learning episodes allowed me to see the teaching profession from a perspective that I had not fully considered before. What I initially thought would be mostly about watching classroom discussions became an experience of paying attention to the small details that influence what happens inside and outside the classroom. From the school environment to the different personalities and circumstances of learners, I discovered that there is often more behind a student's behavior or performance than what can be noticed at first glance. These experiences reminded me that, as a future teacher, I should learn to observe carefully, avoid making immediate judgments, and consider situations from the learners' perspective before deciding how to respond.

Accomplishing the different worksheet activities also encouraged me to become more critical of what I observed instead of simply accepting that a teaching practice was effective because the class appeared attentive or the activity was completed. There were moments when I found myself thinking about what I would have done differently, especially when considering students who needed further assistance or opportunities to participate. I also developed greater respect for my resource teacher after seeing how much thought and effort went into decisions that students might not even notice, from preparing classroom activities to responding to unexpected situations and managing responsibilities throughout the school day. More than anything, I learned that there is no single approach that will work perfectly in every classroom, and recognizing when something needs improvement is part of becoming a better teacher.

As I reach the final part of this e-portfolio, I feel grateful for the opportunity to experience what it means to be in a classroom not merely as a student, but as someone preparing to take on the responsibility of teaching others. Field Study 1 did not make the profession seem easier; instead, it made me more aware of its demands and the areas in which I still need to improve. I know that I have yet to experience the full responsibility of handling my own class, but the observations, interviews, and activities have given me a more realistic view of what awaits me. As a future mathematics educator, I want to carry these realizations into my next teaching experiences and continue developing the judgment, skills, and professional values necessary to make thoughtful decisions for my learners. I consider this experience not simply the completion of another academic requirement, but a meaningful reminder that becoming a teacher is a continuous process of learning from others, examining one's own practices, and striving to do better.
